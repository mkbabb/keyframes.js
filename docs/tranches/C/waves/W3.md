# C.W3 — The demo dogfoods the engine (inv ζ)

The shop-window runs on its own engine. keyframes.js' demo is the product's
storefront, yet it imports its own physics/interpolation engines in exactly ONE
of seven motion surfaces — and even that one hand-rolls the rAF lifecycle the
engine ships (`RAFPlayback.drive`). The reduced-motion path the engine ships
(`prefersReducedMotion()` + the one `withReducedMotion` gate, consulted by all
five play paths) is honored NOWHERE in the demo — `grep -rn "respectReducedMotion:"
demo/` and `grep -rn "prefers-reduced-motion" demo/` both return zero (the π
animation-timing probe B skipped is still open). W3 transposes the seven
hand-rolled rAF loops onto the light engines, honors reduced-motion at the
`defaultAnimationOptions` source, restores the cross-scene swap by *dogfooding*
(an engine-driven class toggle on a real element, NOT a bare `<Transition>` that
re-breaks async scene loading — B.W3's root cause), removes the 18 lines of dead
`.scene-*` CSS, and lands the inv ζ grep gate. This both showcases the engine AND
gives several light surfaces (`SmoothProgress`/`SpringProgress`/`Timeline`/
`RAFPlayback`) their ≥2nd consumer, closing the overfitting bar. Grounds:
`audit/design-findings.txt` motion-dogfood (findings 1–8) + layout-rhythm
(findings 1, 3); `audit/plan-findings.txt` PART 2 finding 3 + deferred-ledger #6.

## § Goal

**What lands:** the demo carries no hand-rolled rAF loop that a shipped light
engine already is. The three true engine-debt transpositions land
(`AnimationVisualizer` coast → `SmoothProgress`+`SpringProgress`; the spring
showcase loop → `RAFPlayback.loop`; the easing ping-pong → `NumericAnimation`
`direction: "alternate"`), the one lifecycle-dedup lands
(`useRafLoop` → `RAFPlayback.loop`), and the two legitimate non-engine rAF sites
(the Three.js present loop in `AmigaScene`; the one-shot post-paint schedulers in
`useTransformState`/`useTimeline`) are recorded as justified exceptions.
Reduced-motion is honored in the demo: `respectReducedMotion: true` enters
`defaultAnimationOptions` so every engine-driven scene inherits the snap-to-rest
path, and the two CSS/loop motions that bypass the engine (the cube `idle-bob`,
the engine-driven scene-swap) consult the engine's own authority. The cross-scene
swap is restored — driven by the engine's reduced-motion-aware `SpringProgress`
animating a class on a real element, not a `<Transition>` wrapper — and the 18
lines of dead `.scene-*` CSS are deleted. A grep gate (inv ζ) counts demo
`requestAnimationFrame` against the justified exceptions.

**Why:** the demo IS the showcase — a shop-window that hand-rolls the very loops
it ships the engine to replace is advertising against itself, and the
reduced-motion path it ships but never honors is the motion-dogfood CRITICAL.
Dogfooding is simultaneously (a) the better showcase, (b) the ≥2nd-consumer the
overfitting precept demands for `SmoothProgress`/`SpringProgress`/`Timeline`/
`RAFPlayback` (each currently has only internal callers), and (c) the
correctness win — `SpringProgress`'s analytic decay replaces a hand-tuned
friction coast, `NumericAnimation(alternate)` replaces hand-synced ping-pong
math, the `_gen` generation-guard replaces a double-schedule-prone demo loop.
One transposition, three findings closed.

## § Scope

### S1 — Transpose the 7 hand-rolled rAF loops onto the light engines
**What:** retire every demo rAF loop that is a literal re-implementation of a
shipped light engine; record the two that legitimately are not.

- **`AnimationVisualizer` coast → `SmoothProgress` + `SpringProgress`.**
  `demo/@/components/custom/animation-controls/controls/AnimationVisualizer.vue`
  hand-rolls (a) exponential velocity smoothing `velocity = velocity*0.6 +
  instantV*0.4` (`AnimationVisualizer.vue:121`) — textbook `SmoothProgress`
  exponential smoothing — and (b) a friction-decay coast loop
  `velocity *= FRICTION; coastProgress += velocity*dt` with its own
  `coastRafId = requestAnimationFrame(coast)` (`:102-103` `FRICTION = 0.92`,
  `:104-105` `VELOCITY_EPSILON`, `:127-168` the coast). Replace the velocity
  estimator with `SmoothProgress` and the release coast with a `SpringProgress`
  seated to the boundary, driven by `RAFPlayback.drive(spring, () =>
  setBallProgress(spring.value))` (`playback.ts:142` `drive(tickable, onFrame)`;
  `playback.ts:30-34` the `Tickable` contract; `playback.ts:151` auto-stops on
  `tickable.settled`). The `Tickable` driver re-arms idempotently on re-seat,
  deleting `stopCoast`/`VELOCITY_EPSILON`/`coastRafId` bookkeeping.
  **Why:** the showcase's own scrubber ignores the engine's two flagship light
  trackers — the single most legible dogfooding win (the primary control surface
  becomes a live `SpringProgress`/`SmoothProgress` advertisement and the inertia
  gets the analytic solver's correctness for free). motion-dogfood finding 2
  [HIGH].

- **The spring showcase loop → `RAFPlayback.loop`.** `demo/spring/useSpringDemo.ts`
  is the ONE scene that instantiates light engines (`SpringProgress` at `:60,73`,
  `NumericAnimation` at `:106`) yet owns a raw `let rafId; requestAnimationFrame(tick)`
  (`:119-158`) calling `t.spring.tick(dt)` manually (`:140`) and re-deriving
  `const dtMs = lastT ? now - lastT : 16.667` (`:128`) — byte-identical to
  `drive`'s own dt seed (`playback.ts:147`). The comparison row genuinely needs
  ONE shared clock to stay phase-aligned (the `:43` comment is right), so this is
  the case for `RAFPlayback.loop(cb)` (`playback.ts:168` — own a single
  `RAFPlayback`, `loop` it, step each tracker inside via `tickDt`). The composable
  stops re-deriving the dt seed and inherits the `_gen` generation-guard
  (`playback.ts:73,170,177`) the demo's hand loop lacks.
  **Why:** the engine built `Tickable`/`drive`/`loop` precisely for
  `SpringProgress`/`SmoothProgress`, and `SpringProgress` already implements
  `Tickable`. The spring scene + the `AnimationVisualizer` transposition give
  `RAFPlayback` its two independent EXTERNAL consumers — the ≥2nd-consumer
  pressure that closes the overfitting bar (`drive`/`loop` currently have only
  internal callers). motion-dogfood finding 3 [HIGH].

- **The easing ping-pong → `NumericAnimation(alternate)`.** `demo/easing/useEasingDemo.ts`
  computes `const phase = cycle % 2; progress = phase <= 1 ? phase : 2 - phase`
  (`:138-139`) over a manual `requestAnimationFrame(tick)` (`:141,148,176,184`) —
  this is exactly `direction: "alternate"` interpolation, which `NumericAnimation`
  provides. The scene already holds `currentEasingFn` as a callable
  `TimingFunction` (`:65`) and already builds a `dummyAnimation` with
  `direction: "alternate"` + `timingFunction: currentEasingFn.value` (`:266-274`)
  purely to satisfy the bottom-bar contract. Drive the preview with a real
  `NumericAnimation([{p:0},{p:1}], { timingFunction: currentEasingFn, direction:
  "alternate", ... })` (`numeric.ts` zero-alloc keyframe interp) whose
  `timingFunction` re-seats from `currentEasingFn` on change — deleting the
  `dummyAnimation` shim AND the manual ping-pong math (`:138-139`). Turn the
  string selector into the typed `Easing` via the boundary-correct
  `resolveEasing(name)` (`index.ts:53` re-exports it) instead of reaching into
  value.js `timingFunctions` directly.
  **Why:** the easing scene demonstrating named curves should itself be powered by
  the engine that resolves them; the hand-synced `progress` + separate
  `dummyAnimation` is the engine re-derived twice. motion-dogfood finding 4 [HIGH].

- **`useRafLoop` → `RAFPlayback.loop` (lifecycle-dedup).**
  `demo/@/components/custom/animation-controls/composables/useRafLoop.ts`
  re-implements a guarded self-rescheduling loop (`loop` →
  `requestAnimationFrame(loop)` with a post-callback guard re-check, `:19-29`)
  that mirrors `RAFPlayback.loop(cb)`'s "callback returns continue" contract
  (`playback.ts:168-187`) MINUS the generation-counter restart-safety the engine
  added (`_gen`, `playback.ts:73,170,177`) — so the demo's version can
  double-schedule on rapid start/stop, the exact bug `RAFPlayback._gen` was
  introduced to fix. Rebase `useRafLoop` onto `RAFPlayback.loop`, wrapping it in
  the Vue lifecycle adapter (`onUnmounted(() => playback.stop())`,
  `playback.ts:191` `_gen++` on stop). The composable becomes a thin reactive skin
  over the engine driver.
  **Why:** two rAF lifecycle implementations collapse to one, and the demo
  inherits the restart-correctness the library already paid for. design-findings
  motion-dogfood finding 7 [LOW]; plan-findings PART 2 finding 3.

- **Record the two justified exceptions.** `demo/app/scenes/AmigaScene.vue:` owns
  `requestAnimationFrame(animate)` for `controls.update()` + `renderer.render()` —
  the WebGL present loop, legitimately NOT a keyframes.js concern (the actual
  sphere animation IS engine-driven via `AnimationGroup` in `useAmigaAnimations`).
  `demo/@/components/custom/matrix-editor/useTransformState.ts:205`
  (`requestAnimationFrame(() => updateTransformations())`, a write-coalescing
  debounce) and `demo/@/components/custom/animation-controls/timeline/composables/
  useTimeline.ts:206` (`requestAnimationFrame(() => resolve())`, await one paint
  before snapshot) are single-frame post-paint schedulers, not animation loops —
  the engine has no "run once next frame" surface and shouldn't grow one.
  **Why:** these are correct uses of raw rAF as a microtask-after-paint
  primitive; recording the rationale prevents the inv ζ gate from mis-firing on
  them and prevents a future tranche mis-filing them as engine debt.
  motion-dogfood findings 5 [MED] + 8 [LOW].

### S2 — Reduced-motion honored in the demo (motion-dogfood CRITICAL)
**What:** the engine ships the gate (`prefersReducedMotion()` +
`withReducedMotion`, `internal/reduced-motion.ts:23,44`; `RAFPlayback.play({
respectReducedMotion })`, `playback.ts:91-134`; `AnimationOptions.respectReducedMotion`,
`constants.ts`) but the demo honors it NOWHERE — proof of absence:
`grep -rn "respectReducedMotion:" demo/` → 0 hits, `grep -rn
"prefers-reduced-motion" demo/` → 0 hits (no JS guard, no CSS guard). Wire it in
at the source:

- Set `respectReducedMotion: true` in `defaultAnimationOptions`
  (`demo/@/components/custom/animation-controls/stores/animationOptionsStore.ts:33-40`
  — the block currently omits the flag, so it defaults `false` for every scene)
  so every engine-driven scene inherits the snap-to-rest path for free.
- Gate the two hand-rolled motions that bypass the engine: wrap the always-on
  cube `idle-bob` (`demo/cube/CubeTarget.vue:134`,
  `animation: idle-bob 3s var(--ease-standard) infinite alternate`) in
  `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` (the CSS twin of the engine
  gate), and short-circuit the S1 light-engine demo loops by consulting the
  engine's exported `prefersReducedMotion()` to snap each tracker to its target
  instead of animating (dogfood the same authority the heavy path uses).
- The `infinite alternate` amiga rotations (`demo/amiga/useAmigaAnimations.ts:57,73,95`)
  run through engine `AnimationGroup` play paths, so they inherit the snap once
  `respectReducedMotion: true` is the default — no per-rotation edit.

**Why:** the demo is the engine's own reduced-motion authority animating its own
shop-window. A user who asked the OS to stop motion currently gets the cube
`idle-bob` and the amiga rotations unconditionally — the motion-dogfood CRITICAL.
One detector, fanned out — exactly the constellation posture the gate's
doc-comment promises, and the runtime confirmation of W2's rest-position
contract that the π animation-timing probe (C.W1) asserts.
design-findings motion-dogfood finding 1 [CRITICAL].

### S3 — Restore the cross-scene swap via the engine + remove dead `.scene-*` CSS
**What:** B.W3 removed the `<Transition mode="out-in">` + `<KeepAlive>` because
wrapping a keyed `<Suspense>` whose child is a `defineAsyncComponent` NEVER
triggered the async loader (documented empirically at `demo/app/App.vue:103-117`)
— shipping a BLANK viewport on amiga/square/easing/spring (B.W3's headline
blocker). Scenes now hard-cut and the swap CSS is orphaned. Restore the swap by
dogfooding, NOT by re-adding a bare `<Transition>`:

- On `activeSceneKey` change (`App.vue:193`), drive an opacity/scale fade with the
  engine's own `SpringProgress` (`src/animation/spring.ts`) bound to a style on
  the `<Suspense>`'s wrapper `<div>` (the host at `App.vue:118`): `spring.play((v)
  => sceneOpacity.value = v)` 0→1, with `style="opacity: v; transform:
  scale(lerp(0.97, 1, v))"`. The async loader stays on the BARE `<Suspense>` —
  untouched — because the spring drives a SIBLING style binding, not a Vue
  `<Transition>` wrapper, so the async-load re-break cannot recur. Use the
  canonical `smooth` spring preset (no overshoot) for a calm enter; the leave is
  the new scene fading in over the old (cross-dissolve via stacked layers) so
  there is never a blank gap.
- Construct the spring with `respectReducedMotion: true` (S2): under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` the spring's snap path jumps to terminal in one emit,
  giving a clean instant swap with zero extra code — the engine's own
  reduced-motion authority animating its own scene-swap.
- DELETE the dead `.scene-enter-active`/`.scene-leave-active`/`.scene-enter-from`/
  `.scene-leave-to` rules at `demo/app/App.vue:407-424` (18 lines) — they are a
  tombstone (`grep -rn 'name="scene"' demo/` → 0 hits; Vue only emits those
  classes when a `<Transition name="scene">` wraps the toggled element).

**Why:** one transposition closes three findings — it fixes the UX regression
(scenes hard-cut), dogfoods the engine (the scene-swap is an OUTPUT of the engine
the demo demonstrates), AND honors reduced-motion (the spring's own snap path).
It is the constellation's resolved decision (C.md § Resolved decision 3): the swap
is restored BY dogfooding, not by re-adding the `<Transition>` that B removed for
cause. design-findings layout-rhythm finding 1 [HIGH]; plan-findings
deferred-ledger #6 [HIGH] + PART 3 finding 6.

### S4 — The inv ζ gate (grep demo rAF against the justified exceptions)
**What:** land a gate that counts demo `requestAnimationFrame` against the
justified-exception allowlist and FAILS if an unjustified raw rAF loop appears.
The exceptions are precisely: the Three.js present loop in
`demo/app/scenes/AmigaScene.vue` (the WebGL renderer, S1) and the two one-shot
post-paint schedulers in `demo/@/components/custom/matrix-editor/useTransformState.ts`
and `demo/@/components/custom/animation-controls/timeline/composables/useTimeline.ts`
(S1). Everything else routes through the engine. The gate greps demo source
(excluding `dist/` — the git-ignored build output that pollutes naive greps,
plan-findings PART 3 finding 13) for `requestAnimationFrame`, subtracts the
allowlist, and asserts zero residual; the allowlist is an explicit, reviewable
manifest (a file-path set), so adding a raw rAF requires a deliberate
allowlist edit, not a silent slip.

**Why:** inv ζ ("the shop-window runs on its own engine") needs a standing
instrument, mirroring inv α's `proof:boundary` posture — the demo carries no
hand-rolled rAF loop that a shipped light engine already is, and the grep is the
gate that keeps it true on every future demo edit. The justified exceptions are
enumerated (not waved through) so the gate bites on a real regression but does
not mis-fire on the WebGL present loop or the post-paint schedulers.
design-findings motion-dogfood § synthesis (3 transpositions + 1 dedup + 2
RECORD); inv ζ (C.md § Inherited invariants).

## § Hard gate

- **inv ζ bites.** The grep gate counts demo `requestAnimationFrame` (excluding
  `dist/`) and FAILS on any raw rAF loop outside the three-file justified-exception
  allowlist (`AmigaScene.vue`, `useTransformState.ts`, `useTimeline.ts`). A
  mutation test confirms the bite: adding a bare `requestAnimationFrame` loop to a
  non-allowlisted demo file reddens the gate; the count for the seven pre-W3 files
  drops to the three allowlisted ones. The gate must BITE, not merely log.

- **The light surfaces gain their ≥2nd EXTERNAL consumer.** `grep -rn "new
  SmoothProgress\|new SpringProgress\|RAFPlayback" demo/` (excluding `dist/`)
  shows `AnimationVisualizer` (SmoothProgress + SpringProgress + RAFPlayback.drive),
  `useSpringDemo` (RAFPlayback.loop), and the scene-swap (SpringProgress) as
  independent external callers — closing the overfitting bar
  (`SmoothProgress`/`SpringProgress`/`RAFPlayback` were internal-only before W3).
  The easing scene shows `new NumericAnimation` driving the preview (the
  `dummyAnimation` shim deleted).

- **Reduced-motion is honored.** `grep -rn "respectReducedMotion" demo/`
  (excluding `dist/`) is non-empty (was 0): `defaultAnimationOptions` carries
  `respectReducedMotion: true`, and `grep -rn "prefers-reduced-motion" demo/`
  shows the cube `idle-bob` CSS guard. A reduced-motion runtime check (the C.W1 π
  animation-timing probe under `prefers-reduced-motion`) confirms the spring
  scene-swap and the engine-driven scenes snap to their rest frame in one emit
  rather than animating.

- **The dead CSS is gone and the swap is restored.** `grep -rn "scene-enter\|scene-leave"
  demo/app/App.vue` → 0 hits (the 18 lines deleted); the keyed `<Suspense>` at
  `App.vue:118` still resolves each scene's async chunk (no blank-scene
  regression — the B.W3 blocker does not recur), and the scene-swap fade is driven
  by `SpringProgress` on a sibling style binding, NOT a `<Transition>` wrapper.

- **No new workaround / legacy / alias.** The transpositions are net-deletion (the
  hand-rolled coast/ping-pong/velocity-smoothing/`useRafLoop` bodies are removed,
  not kept beside the engine calls); no `dummyAnimation` shim, no
  `VELOCITY_EPSILON`/`coastRafId`/`FRICTION` bookkeeping, no re-derived dt seed
  survives.

## § Design decisions

- **The scene-swap is restored by dogfooding, not by re-adding a bare
  `<Transition>` (RESOLVED, C.md decision 3).** B removed the `<Transition
  mode="out-in">` because it broke async scene loading — wrapping a keyed
  `<Suspense>` over a `defineAsyncComponent` never fired the loader (App.vue:103-117).
  The transposition drives the fade with `SpringProgress` on a SIBLING style
  binding to the `<Suspense>` host (`App.vue:118`), leaving the async boundary
  untouched. Trade-off: a sibling-style fade cannot use Vue's
  `enter/leave` lifecycle hooks, so the "leave" is a cross-dissolve (new scene
  fades in over the old via stacked layers) rather than a true out-then-in — but
  this is the correct trade: it is the ONLY restoration that does not re-break the
  async loader, and the cross-dissolve has no blank gap (strictly better than the
  current hard-cut). One transposition closes the UX regression + the dogfood gap
  + the reduced-motion gap.

- **Reduced-motion is wired at the `defaultAnimationOptions` source, not
  per-scene.** Setting `respectReducedMotion: true` once in the store
  (`animationOptionsStore.ts:33-40`) fans the snap-to-rest path to every
  engine-driven scene for free (the amiga rotations, all `AnimationGroup` plays).
  Only the two motions that BYPASS the engine — the cube `idle-bob` CSS animation
  and the S1 light-engine demo loops — need an explicit gate (the CSS `@media`
  twin and a `prefersReducedMotion()` short-circuit). Trade-off: this changes the
  demo's default motion posture for reduced-motion users (motion stops where it
  previously ran) — which is precisely the correct, OS-honoring behavior the
  engine ships and the demo must model. One detector, fanned out.

- **`RAFPlayback.loop` for the spring comparison row, `drive` for the
  visualizer.** The spring showcase needs ONE shared clock so the three trackers
  stay phase-aligned (`useSpringDemo.ts:43`), so it owns a single `RAFPlayback`
  and `loop`s it, stepping each tracker inside — not three independent `drive`
  calls (which would phase-drift). The visualizer's single coasting ball uses
  `drive` (one `Tickable`, settle-based auto-stop). Trade-off: `loop` requires the
  consumer to own the per-frame step body (the demo steps each tracker via
  `tickDt`), where `drive` owns it — but `loop` is the right shape for the
  multi-tracker shared-clock case the comparison row genuinely is, and using both
  shapes from two scenes proves the `RAFPlayback` API across its surface, not just
  one entry. (The engine-side `drive`/`loop` generation-guard convergence is
  C.W4's residual; W3 consumes the API as shipped.)

- **The justified exceptions are enumerated, not waved.** inv ζ's gate carries an
  explicit three-file allowlist (Three.js present loop + two post-paint
  schedulers) rather than a blanket "demo rAF is fine" — so the gate bites on a
  real new hand-rolled loop while never mis-firing on the WebGL present loop (a
  legitimate non-keyframes concern) or the microtask-after-paint primitives (which
  the engine deliberately does not provide). The allowlist is reviewable: adding a
  raw rAF demands a deliberate manifest edit.

- **`resolveEasing(name)` is the boundary-correct selector→`Easing` conversion.**
  The easing scene's `NumericAnimation` re-seats its `timingFunction` from the
  typed `Easing` that `resolveEasing(name)` (`index.ts:53`) returns, rather than
  reaching into value.js `timingFunctions` directly — keeping the demo on the
  engine's public, boundary-proven surface (the same `resolveEasing` the light
  path uses), not the value.js internals.
