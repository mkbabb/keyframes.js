# Pane Audit — Easing Scene
**Tranche J · Design Audit · Lane: pane-easing**

---

## Overall Verdict

The easing scene is the most mathematically pure of all demo scenes — the bezier curve IS the product — and the current state is largely coherent. The I5/W11 stage-card registration landed cleanly (the `<Card>` protagonist plate is properly rounded, the cartoon-quiet panels recede correctly, the two-altitude hierarchy holds). However, several hierarchy and suffusion issues remain:

1. **The subject (the hero ball) is the scene's protagonist but fails to project that hierarchy.** At all breakpoints the hero ball is a featureless green circle resting motionlessly in the center-right of a large near-empty glass stage. It occupies roughly 4% of the stage area; the remaining 96% is blank. The curve IS the brand — its motion is the product — but there is no visual relationship between the ball's trajectory and the mathematical object being edited. The curve canvas in the sidebar is the richest visual element in the scene yet it is confined to a narrow ~400px rail. The eye reads the two-panel split as two equal things rather than as a sidebar-subordinate protagonist.

2. **The easing name / readout bar (top of stage) is typographically timid.** `ease F(0.35) = 0.612` is rendered in `text-heading` (system sans) + `text-mono-caption`, both at a small scale flush with the top-left corner of the stage card. This is the only named moment for the current curve — the whole scene's identity — and it is visually subordinate to the stage chrome, not dominant over it.

3. **The stage is too empty.** The stage card from laptop upward has ~600–900px of vertical white space with one ball floating in the middle. Generous negative space can be intentional, but here it reads as unfinished rather than deliberate — there is no anchoring geometry (no subtle grid, no mathematical motif) to give it proportioned breath.

4. **Color distribution is flat.** The green ball at ~56px carries the only accent color against an off-white stage. The curve itself (in the sidebar) is purple/indigo (`--ppmycota-primary`). These two brand colors don't communicate — the ball is green (progress token) and the curve is purple (brand), but on stage the purple is invisible; the ball is the only moving signal. The colors serve different registries with no relationship established.

5. **Mobile is well-composed relative to desktop** — the easing editor fills the viewport top-to-bottom, the ball is visible at the bottom, and the canvas is large relative to the screen. The open state on mobile is unchanged (the controls panel IS the scene on mobile), which is correct.

## Open/Closed Panel Composition Assessment

**Mobile (375w):** The closed state shows the curve canvas + dropdown + duration slider filling the full viewport below the title bar — the EasingEditor dominates appropriately. The "Easing Preview" dock bar at the bottom is compact and correct. Opening the panel (mobile-open.png) shows no visual change because on mobile the sidebar IS the full scene — no separate stage visible. This is correct: the controls panel on mobile is the protagonist. One issue: the `F(0.96) = 0.999` readout in the header bar is shown even on mobile-open, which means the header still reads `ease  F(0.96) = 0.999  Singular ›` while the controls panel shows the same curve — redundant information at two levels.

**Laptop/Desktop:** The closed state (not pictured — closed panel would collapse the rail) and open state are effectively the same viewport since `storedControls.isControlsPanelOpen = true` is forced in `EasingScene.vue:37`. The panel is ALWAYS OPEN on the easing scene. This means the stage's 900px wide glass card always competes with the ~400px controls rail. The two-column composition reads correctly (sidebar left, stage right), but the stage's emptiness relative to the sidebar's density creates an imbalance. The sidebar is the richer object; the stage should be the protagonist but currently reads as the emptier secondary area.

The forced-open behavior is intentional (the easing scene has no keyframes/timeline to switch to), but a consequence is that the easing scene NEVER shows the expanded stage (which other scenes can reach by closing the panel). Consider whether a "minimal mode" that hides the curve canvas when collapsed to just the dropdown + readout would allow a full-stage hero moment for the ball.
